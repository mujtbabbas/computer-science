# Reader's Guide — computer-science (OSSU)

## What this project is

This is the **Open Source Society University (OSSU) Computer Science
curriculum**: a complete path to a **free, self-taught education in computer
science**, built entirely from free online courses offered by respected
universities. Followed in order, it approximates the requirements of an
undergraduate CS degree — with prerequisites, a full core, advanced tracks and
a final project — at zero tuition cost.

## How the content is organised

- [`README.md`](README.md) — the curriculum itself:
  - **Summary** and **Community** — what the path is, and how to join the
    learner community.
  - **Curriculum** — the course sequence, in order:
    - **Prerequisites** — what you need before starting.
    - **Intro CS** — the opening courses.
    - **Core CS** — the degree core, in strands: *Core programming*,
      *Core math*, *CS Tools*, *Core systems*, *Core theory*,
      *Core security*, *Core applications* and *Core ethics*.
    - **Advanced CS** — deeper tracks: *Advanced programming*,
      *Advanced systems*, *Advanced theory*, *Advanced Information Security*
      and *Advanced math*.
    - **Final project** — a capstone to consolidate the degree.
  - **Code of conduct** and **How to show your progress** — community rules
    and ways to track/share your journey.
- `coursepages/` — dedicated pages for individual courses in the curriculum.
- `extras/` — supplementary material around the main path.
- [`FAQ.md`](FAQ.md) — questions every prospective student asks;
  [`HELP.md`](HELP.md) — where to get help; `CURRICULAR_GUIDELINES.md` — the
  principles the curriculum is built and reviewed against;
  `CONTRIBUTING.md` — how curriculum changes are proposed.
- The repo also renders as a website (Jekyll: `_config.yml`, `_layouts/`,
  `_includes/`, `CNAME`).

## How to use it

1. Read the README's **Summary** and [`FAQ.md`](FAQ.md) to understand the
   commitment (it is a degree-sized path, paced in years, not weeks).
2. Check **Prerequisites**, then start at **Intro CS** and work down the
   **Curriculum** in order — courses within a strand are sequenced on purpose.
3. Use `coursepages/` for the detail page of the course you are on, and the
   README's progress section to log your advancement.
